The councillor's bike lane scheme is an attack on everything we stand for

Opinion: City Hall wants to take your parking space. Our columnist asks the questions they won't.

By Rex Grumbly · Columnist

Councillor Maya Ortiz wants to put protected bike lanes on Main Street. Before we discuss the plan, readers should know that Ortiz failed her driving test twice, so it's hardly surprising she wants to punish people who can drive.

The councillor says the lanes would make the street safer for children cycling to school. In other words, she thinks anyone who drives a car is a reckless menace who doesn't care whether children live or die.

Main Street has had four lanes of traffic since 1962. It has always been this way, and there is no reason to change something that has served this town for generations.

A war on drivers

This scheme is nothing less than a war on hard-working drivers, cooked up by a radical clique of latte-sipping zealots who want to dictate how ordinary people live.

Either we keep every parking space on Main Street, or the shops close and the town dies. Those are the choices.

Ortiz points to a study showing that bike lanes increased spending in three other cities. But those cities are nothing like ours, and in any case, the study was paid for by a cycling charity, so we can safely throw it out.

"No real local would ever ride a bicycle down Main Street in February."

When I raised these concerns at the last council meeting, the councillor's supporters pointed out that I park illegally outside my own office. That may be true, but what about the councillor's own expenses claims? She is hardly one to lecture anyone about rules.

Since the cycle lanes opened on Harbour Road last year, two shops on that street have closed. The lanes are obviously driving businesses away.

The plan is wrong because it is a bad idea, and it is a bad idea because it is wrong for this town.

Have you stopped to ask why the councillor is so desperate to rush this through before the election?
