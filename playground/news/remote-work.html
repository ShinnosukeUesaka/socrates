Remote work is quietly destroying civilisation, experts say

Productivity, families and the very fabric of society are at stake as millions refuse to return to the office.

By Gordon Sure · Lifestyle Correspondent · 6 min read

It has been four years since the great experiment began, and the verdict is in. Experts say that working from home is the single biggest threat to the modern economy, and frankly, anyone who has spent a week in pyjamas already knows it.

Ever since companies started allowing remote work, the national birth rate has fallen, so it is clear that working from home is tearing families apart. The timing speaks for itself.

"I tried working from home for a month and I got nothing done," says Brian, a 52-year-old regional sales manager from Dunmore. "So it obviously doesn't work for anyone." Brian's experience is typical of what we heard from readers.

A slippery road

Make no mistake about where this is heading. If we let employees stay home on Fridays, next thing you know they will want Mondays too, then the whole week, and before long our city centres will be ghost towns overrun by crime.

Productivity has fallen by 12 percent, according to figures shared with this newspaper. That number alone should end the debate.

"Either we bring everyone back to the office five days a week, or we accept the collapse of company culture."

That stark choice, put to us by a chief executive who asked not to be named, sums up the situation. There is simply no middle ground.

Supporters of remote work like to claim that commuting is a waste of time. So what they are really saying is that they don't care about their colleagues, their managers or the businesses that pay their salaries.

What the defenders won't tell you

Of course, the loudest defenders of remote work are young tech workers who have never had a real job, so their opinions on the matter can safely be ignored.

Offices have been the heart of working life for more than a hundred years. If it worked for our grandparents, it is good enough for us.

Nobody has ever proven that remote workers are actually working, so we can only assume they are not. No true professional would choose the sofa over the boardroom.

Think of the children growing up in homes where a parent is always on a video call. Do we really want a generation raised by laptops?

The message from the experts is clear, and the only sensible response is to act now before it is too late.
