City debates new bike lanes on Main Street

By A. Reporter · October 24, 2026

The city council met on Tuesday evening to discuss a proposal that would add protected bike lanes along a two-mile stretch of Main Street, replacing one lane of parking on each side.

Councillor Grey opposed the plan. "If the city allows one more bike lane, next thing you know cars will be banned from downtown entirely," she said during the public comment period.

Supporters pointed to a traffic study commissioned last year, which counted cyclists and pedestrians at twelve intersections over a period of six weeks and recommended separated lanes on the busiest routes.

The council is expected to hold a second reading of the proposal next month, after the transportation department publishes its revised cost estimates and a schedule for construction that would minimise disruption for local businesses.

Several business owners on Main Street said they were still weighing the trade-offs. Some worried about deliveries and customer parking during construction, while others said more foot traffic would help them.

The transportation department said it would hold two open houses before the second reading, where residents can look at drawings of the proposed lanes and leave written comments for the council.

A local cycling group has organised a ride along the route this weekend to show council members what the street is like for people on bikes during the morning rush.

Regional transit officials said the bus routes on Main Street would not change under the current version of the plan, though stop locations could move slightly.

A petition in favour of the lanes has gathered thousands of signatures. "Millions of cyclists can't be wrong about this," the organiser wrote in a letter to the council.

The meeting adjourned shortly after ten o'clock.
